## What are Ryan's strengths?
The first is understanding what other teams need: he starts from the people who know the workflow and
delivers with the data he has, like the view of disqualified leads he built for the sales manager. The
second is learning from mistakes: on the CDC replication, his first attempt failed because he had not
researched the documentation; with his manager's guidance he rebuilt it the right way, and now he
researches before building.

## What is Ryan still working on?
The more technical side and architecture: knowing which architecture and technology are best for each
situation, and how to plan the rollout of a large system, like the billing one. He works on this through
examples, practice at work and study on his own.

## How does Ryan work in a team?
For Ryan, working in a team is better, especially early in a career: each colleague knows more about one
part, and he learns from each of them what he will use in his next tasks. He likes to split the work,
each person with one part, always aligning with the people around him, to deliver faster and avoid
bottlenecks.

## How does Ryan handle tight deadlines or pressure?
Ryan tries to deliver within the planned deadline. If he sees it will not work, he says so early and asks
for help to deliver as fast as possible, depending on how important the delivery is.

## How does Ryan react when he makes a mistake?
Ryan treats mistakes as part of the job. When something goes wrong, he investigates the cause, researches,
fixes it or rolls the change back, and rethinks the solution before trying again.
